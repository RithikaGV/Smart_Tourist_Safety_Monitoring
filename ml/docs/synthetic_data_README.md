# Synthetic Datasets — Movement, Crowd Density, Crime Score

## What this is

Three datasets generated by `generate_synthetic_data.py`, seeded on the
real `nilgiris_places.csv` place-graph (not random noise) so patterns are
geographically plausible. Already run once — outputs below are ready to
use, but you can re-run the script anytime (e.g. after Colab gives you
verified coordinates) with `python3 generate_synthetic_data.py`.

**These are synthetic. Say so, always, wherever these numbers appear —**
in the Phase 15 docs, in any demo, in any evaluation claim. Do not present
model accuracy on this data as validated real-world performance.

---

## 1. tourist_movements.csv (13,553 rows, 1,000 tourists)

Each row is one visit-event: which tourist, which place, when, how long
they stayed, distance from the previous stop.

**Generation logic:**
- Each tourist starts from a synthetic "Hotel Zone" node in their home
  cluster (Ooty-weighted at 45%, matching real entry-point dominance —
  most Nilgiris tourism funnels through Ooty).
- 3 simulated days, 3–6 stops per day, always returning to the hotel
  each evening.
- Next-stop choice is a weighted random walk: prefers staying within
  the current cluster, weighted by place popularity (lakes/gardens/
  waterfalls score higher than villages/forests) — this mirrors real
  tourist behavior (concentrate on attractions) without claiming to
  model any actual observed tourist.
- **6.5% of tourists (65 of 1000) are flagged `is_suspicious_tourist`**
  and get an elevated probability of choosing restricted-zone nodes.

**Verified signal separation** (sanity-checked after generation):
- Suspicious tourists: 9.6% of their visits are to restricted zones
- Normal tourists: 0.0% of their visits are to restricted zones

This gap is what Modules 4/5/6 (DBSCAN, Isolation Forest, Pattern
Detection) should be able to detect. If a model trained on this data
can't separate these two groups, that's a real bug to debug — the signal
is intentionally clean and strong in v1 so you can validate the modeling
pipeline works before worrying about noisier, more realistic signal
separation later.

## 2. crowd_density.csv (600 rows: 60 places × 2 seasons × 5 hour-buckets)

Synthetic density score (0–100) per place, split by season (peak:
Apr/May/Jun/Oct/Nov/Dec vs. off: the rest) and 5 hour-buckets across the day.

**Generation logic:** base popularity by place type × season multiplier
(1.3x peak, 0.7x off) × hour-of-day multiplier (midday highest) × random
noise (±15%). Restricted zones get an extra 0.3x dampener (permit-gated
access genuinely means lower footfall, not just lower reported crowding).

**Verified:** peak season average density (41.1) is nearly double off
season (22.5), so the seasonal signal is present, not accidentally flat.

---

## 3. crime_score.csv (60 rows, one per place)

Synthetic baseline score (0–10) per place.

**Generation logic:** higher-footfall, urban/village-type places score
higher (more opportunity for petty theft/scams — a defensible real-world
pattern, not arbitrary); remote/forest/restricted places score lower.

**Important interpretation note:** this table is specifically about
*crime opportunity*, not overall danger. A remote restricted zone can have
a low crime_score and still be genuinely dangerous (wildlife, terrain,
isolation) — that combination is what Risk Prediction (Module 1) and
Safety Score (Module 2) are for, by combining this with weather, distance
to help, and disaster risk. Don't let a low crime_score alone imply "safe."
